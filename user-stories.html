1 Overview & conventions

This document is the single source of truth for the customer-facing behaviour of the FHI Internet rate card. It complements the developer documentation: where the dev doc describes how to build, this one captures why and for whom.

The reference implementation ships in template.html as a single-controller AngularJS 1.8.2 module (RateCardCtrl as vm, payload bound to vm.plan). Every “Component” reference inside a story points at a CSS class in that file; every CMS-editable field maps to a single path on vm.plan documented in the dev guide.

How to read a story

Every story follows the canonical Connextra format and is paired with acceptance criteria in Gherkin syntax. Stories are also tagged so the team can plan and slice work.

TagMeaning
MUSTRequired for first launch — non-negotiable for the MVP.
SHOULDHigh value, ship soon after MUST. Skipping must be justified.
COULDNice to have, ship if capacity allows.
SSmall — < 1 dev-day, single file.
MMedium — 1 to 3 dev-days, spans 2–3 files.
LLarge — 3+ dev-days, may cross domains (CMS + front).
Source of truth When a story conflicts with the live preview, the story wins. Update the implementation and notify the PO. When acceptance criteria conflict with the developer documentation, raise the issue in refinement before coding.

Story ID schema

Every story has a stable ID like RC-A-01:

  • RC — Rate Card product area.
  • A…H — Epic letter (see Epics map).
  • 01… — Sequential number within the epic.

IDs are stable across versions. If a story is rewritten, keep the ID and bump the title.

2 Personas

Six personas drive the backlog. They are composite, not real customers, but each maps to a real segment we can measure (analytics cohorts, support tickets, A11y audits, CMS editor logs).

SH
Sarah, "The Shopper"
Comparison cohort · 38% of visits

34, busy parent, comparing 2–3 ISP plans across tabs. Visits the page from a Google search.

GoalsFind the cheapest plan that covers her family's streaming.

FrustrationsHidden long-term costs, marketing fluff, slow pages.

MK
Marc, "The Skeptic"
Price-sensitive · 22% of visits

52, mistrusts promo pricing. Will scroll for fine print before clicking any CTA.

GoalsSee the full price evolution and total cost over 24 months.

Frustrations"Starting at" prices, asterisks without context.

LE
Léa, "The Power User"
Spec-driven · 14% of visits

28, gamer / streamer. Knows what Gbps, latency, and "unlimited" actually mean.

GoalsConfirm bandwidth and data allowance match her usage.

FrustrationsVague speeds, soft data caps disguised as unlimited.

JP

Low digital literacy · 9% of visits

67, retired. Sometimes uses a magnifier or 200% zoom. Calls support after browsing.

GoalsRead a clear single price and a phone number / clear CTA.

4 Epic A · Browse & evaluate

Outcome — A shopper recognises the plan name and current promo price within 3 seconds of the card entering the viewport.

RC-A-01
MUST S
Identify the plan at a glance
As a comparison shopper (Sarah),
I want the plan badge and headline to be the first elements I see,
so that I can confirm I'm looking at the right product in under one second.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given the rate card is rendered with a configured badge and headline,
  • When the card enters the viewport,
  • Then the badge pill is visible at the top of the card with background #17599C and white text.
  • And the headline is rendered below the badge in brand orange #ed6b1f using the Ambra Sans Text font at 40px (desktop) / 32px (mobile).
  • And both are above the fold on a 375×667 viewport.
Component: .plan-badge, .plan-headline Persona: Sarah KPI: Time-to-recognition < 3s
RC-A-02
MUST S
Understand the price at a glance
As a comparison shopper,
I want the discounted price to dominate the card,
so that I can compare it against competitors without reading the surrounding copy.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given a plan with a current price and a regular price,
  • Then the current amount is the largest text in the card (64px desktop / 48px mobile, weight 800).
  • And the "$" sign sits to the left at 32px, top-aligned with a 2px gap.
  • And the period suffix (/mo.) is shown to the right at 16px.
  • And the regular price is rendered above with a strikethrough.
Component: .hero-price Persona: Sarah KPI: Price visibility on heatmap
RC-A-03
SHOULD S
See total promo savings up front
As a price-sensitive shopper,
I want a "Save $X in your first year" badge near the price,
so that I understand the value of the promo without scrolling.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given a savings label is configured,
  • Then the savings pill is rendered under the price block with an orange background #ed6b1f and white text.
  • And the pill contains a piggy-bank pictogram (18×24.75) on the left.
  • And the pill is hidden when the editor leaves the field empty.
Component: .savings-label Persona: Marc

5 Epic B · Pricing transparency

Outcome — A skeptical shopper trusts the promo because they can preview every price step before signing up.

RC-B-01
MUST M
Open the Price Journey breakdown on demand
As Marc the skeptic,
I want to open a focused See price journey panel,
so that I can review every price step from month 1 to the end of the promo without losing sight of the plan I’m considering.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given the editor has flipped “Show ‘See price journey’ link” ON in the CMS,
  • Then a blue underlined link with an orange chevron renders below the CTA.
  • When a user taps or clicks that link,
  • Then a modal opens with a dark backdrop (overlay fades over 0.2s; panel slides up 12px over 0.22s).
  • And the modal header echoes the plan badge (e.g. “Internet 1G — Price journey”); the body shows the same step timeline + summary banner authored in the CMS; the footer recaps the price and offers a duplicated CTA for one-click conversion.
  • And the modal can be closed by clicking ✕, the backdrop, or pressing Escape; the page behind the backdrop cannot scroll while it is open.
  • And the link is hidden by default on first render — it surfaces only when the editor enables it.
Component: .journey-link → .modal-overlay + .modal-panel Persona: Marc
RC-B-02
MUST M
Visualise each pricing period as a step
As a budget-planner,
I want each pricing period rendered as a stand-alone card with period, price, optional tag, and optional sub-text,
so that I can compare them side by side.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given N journey steps are configured,
  • Then each step is rendered as a rounded card (radius 16px) inside .journey-steps.
  • And active / discounted steps use a blue fill (#136BA3 / #0d4d7a) with white text.
  • And regular steps use a grey fill (#f0f1f3) with dark text.
  • And each step displays a period (13px, weight 500), a price (26px, weight 800), and optional sub-text (13px).
  • And each non-first step is preceded by an orange chevron › (27px, #ed6b1f).
Component: .journey-step + .journey-chevron Persona: Marc, Sarah
RC-B-03
SHOULD S
Highlight the current step with a tag
As a curious shopper,
I want a floating pill tag on one of the steps (e.g. "Free trial", "NOW"),
so that I instantly map the displayed price to a moment in time.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given a step has a tag value,
  • Then the tag is rendered as an orange pill (#ed6b1f) floating -12px above the step.
  • And the tag text is 13px / weight 700 / white, with padding 4px 10px.
  • And a step without a tag value renders without any pill.
Component: .step-tag Persona: Sarah, Marc
RC-B-04
SHOULD S
Summarise yearly savings inside the journey header
As Marc,
I want a "Save $X/year" summary inside the Price Journey header,
so that I can quickly grasp the cumulative promo value while reading the breakdown.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given a yearly savings value is configured,
  • Then the journey header renders the savings line on the right with an orange dollar pictogram and the text "Save $X/year".
  • And the savings text is 13px, weight 600, colour #ed6b1f.
  • And when the value is empty the line is hidden but the journey is still rendered.
Component: .journey-header + .journey-savings Persona: Marc
RC-B-05
MUST S
Keep card heights aligned in multi-card layouts
As a content designer placing several rate cards side by side,
I want every card to reserve the same vertical space for the price-journey link,
so that CTAs and legal notes line up across the row — even when only some plans have a journey configured.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given a row of N rate cards in which some plans have a journey and others do not,
  • Then the .journey-link-row is rendered on every card with min-height: 36px and padding: 10px 20px 6px.
  • And the link <button> inside the row is only emitted when journey.enabled === true (otherwise the row stays empty but reserved).
  • And the CTA top edge, journey-link row top edge, and legal note top edge are identical across every card in the row (± 1px sub-pixel rounding).
  • And the spacing rule survives a re-flow when an editor toggles a journey on or off without other layout shifts.
Component: .journey-link-row (reserved space) · see rate-cards-demo.html for the multi-card preview Persona: Christine (PO), Sophie (designer)

9 Epic F · Accessibility

Outcome — Users with assistive technologies or motor / visual impairments can complete the same tasks as sighted mouse users.

RC-F-01
MUST M
Navigate the entire card with the keyboard
As a keyboard-only user,
I want to reach every toggle and the CTA using Tab,
so that I can interact without a mouse.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given a keyboard,
  • Then tabbing visits, in DOM order: the “What’s included” toggle, the CTA, and (when enabled) the “See price journey” link.
  • And each focused element shows a visible focus ring of ≥ 2px with contrast ratio ≥ 3:1 against the background.
  • And pressing Enter or Space on the “What’s included” toggle expands / collapses its panel; pressing it on the journey link opens the modal (see RC-F-05).
Standard: WCAG 2.1 — 2.1.1, 2.4.7 Persona: Aïcha, Jean-Pierre
RC-F-02
MUST M
Hear the card with a screen reader
As Aïcha,
I want every meaningful element announced in logical reading order,
so that I can shop independently.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given VoiceOver / NVDA / TalkBack is active,
  • Then reading order is: badge → headline → original price → current price → savings → specs → “What’s included” toggle → CTA → (optional) “See price journey” link → legal.
  • And decorative SVGs declare aria-hidden="true" (chevron next to the link, summary icon, etc.).
  • And the “What’s included” toggle announces “collapsed” / “expanded” via aria-expanded.
  • And the price-journey link announces “has pop-up dialog” via aria-haspopup="dialog".
  • And the CTA’s accessible name matches its visible label.
Standard: WCAG 2.1 — 1.3.1, 4.1.2 Persona: Aïcha
RC-F-03
SHOULD S
Respect reduced-motion preference
As a motion-sensitive user,
I want panel expansions and CTA hover transitions disabled when I request reduced motion,
so that I avoid vestibular discomfort.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given the OS sets prefers-reduced-motion: reduce,
  • Then the collapsible max-height and background transitions are removed.
  • And the CTA hover is an instant colour swap.
Standard: WCAG 2.1 — 2.3.3 Persona: Aïcha, Jean-Pierre
RC-F-04
MUST S
Reach minimum colour contrast on every text
As any user with low vision,
I want body and price text to keep a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against the background,
so that I can read the card under poor lighting or with cataract.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given the rendered card,
  • Then all text passes 4.5:1 contrast (3:1 for ≥ 18.66px bold).
  • And the legal note (#999 on #fafafa) is the only documented exception → editor warning in the CMS.
Standard: WCAG 2.1 — 1.4.3 Persona: Jean-Pierre
RC-F-05
MUST L
Operate the Price Journey modal without a mouse
As Aïcha (screen-reader user) and Jean-Pierre (keyboard-only user),
I want the price-journey modal to behave like a proper dialog,
so that I can open it, navigate its content, and close it without sight or a pointer.
Acceptance criteria
  • Given the journey link is rendered,
  • Then the link carries aria-haspopup="dialog" and aria-controls="journeyModal".
  • And the modal uses role="dialog", aria-modal="true", and aria-labelledby pointing at its visible title.
  • When the link is activated (Enter, Space, or click),
  • Then focus moves to the ✕ close button within ~50ms.
  • And pressing Escape anywhere on the page closes the modal.
  • And clicking the backdrop closes the modal; clicking inside the panel does NOT.
  • When the modal closes,
  • Then focus returns to the link that opened it (the stored lastTrigger).
  • And the page behind the backdrop never scrolls while the modal is open (body.style.overflow = 'hidden' during the open state).
  • And the overlay’s aria-hidden attribute mirrors the closed state so assistive tech ignores the entire region when shut.
Standard: WCAG 2.1 — 2.1.1, 2.1.2, 2.4.3, 4.1.2 · WAI-ARIA Dialog pattern Persona: Aïcha, Jean-Pierre

12 Definition of Done

A story is "done" only when every box below can be ticked. The PO refuses delivery if any item is unchecked without a written waiver.

Story-level DoD

  • All acceptance criteria pass on Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge (latest two majors).
  • Tested on iOS Safari 16+ and Android Chrome 110+ on a real 375×667 device.
  • Tested with VoiceOver (iOS / macOS) and NVDA (Windows).
  • Keyboard-only walkthrough completed by a non-author.
  • Lighthouse Accessibility score ≥ 95 on the page hosting the card.
  • Visual diff approved by design (Figma frame referenced).
  • Content reviewed by a copywriter (no placeholder text shipped).
  • Telemetry events fired for CTA click, toggle open/close, and Price Journey reveal.
  • Story updated with the actual delivered behaviour if scope changed.

Release-level DoD

  • All MUST stories of all epics shipped and verified.
  • CMS form fields match the vm.plan model paths in template.html (cross-checked against the dev doc §12.7 field map).
  • Default state in production matches story RC-H-03 (Price Journey OFF, features panel closed).
  • Performance budget: card renders < 100 ms on a 4G mid-range device.
  • Bundle: no third-party JS added beyond AngularJS 1.8.2 + angular-sanitize 1.8.2.
  • Documentation pages (this one + developer doc) updated.
  • Release notes published in the internal newsroom channel.

13 Glossary

TermDefinition
Rate cardThe pricing component that advertises a subscription plan (Internet, TV, Phone) with badge, price, specs, features, CTA, and legal note.
Price JourneyThe collapsible breakdown of how the price evolves over time, rendered as side-by-side step cards separated by orange chevrons.
StepA single pricing period inside the Price Journey, with period label, price, optional tag, optional sub-text, and a style (active / discounted / regular).
TagThe floating pill highlight on a step (e.g. "Free trial", "NOW"). Orange background, white text.
Savings labelThe orange pill under the hero price ("Save $X in your first year") that summarises the promo value.
What's includedThe collapsible bullet list of plan benefits, each row with a configurable icon and colour.
CTAThe primary call-to-action button at the bottom of the card.
ConfiguratorThe live preview admin panel on /template.html. Stands in for the production CMS admin form. Powered by AngularJS 1.8.2 two-way binding (ng-model) against vm.plan, so every edit re-renders the card instantly.
vm.planThe single payload object the rate card reads from. Every CMS-editable field is a path on this object (e.g. vm.plan.badge, vm.plan.journey.steps[]). Full schema in the dev doc §12.2.
